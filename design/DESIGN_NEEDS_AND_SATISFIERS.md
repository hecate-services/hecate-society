# Needs and satisfiers: nine axes, none of them first

**Decision: the axes are Max-Neef's nine fundamental human needs, taken whole, and
a culture is a set of satisfiers rather than a weighting over the axes.**

Decided 2026-08-04, from a five-way split proposed as primary, relational, status,
spiritual and expressional. The research found that split was close to a named
framework, and the framework's apparatus is worth more than the list.

---

## The nine

**subsistence, protection, affection, understanding, participation, leisure,
creation, identity, freedom.**

The five that were proposed map onto them: primary is subsistence and protection,
relational is affection and participation, status is largely identity, spiritual is
understanding with identity and freedom, expressional is creation and leisure.

## Three claims adopted with them, each doing real work

### Needs are universal and finite; satisfiers are cultural

Max-Neef's central claim is that the nine are the same in every culture and every
historical period, and that what varies is the *means* by which they are met.

That is **give the plan, evolve the parameters, already formalised by an economist
in 1986.** It also replaces a weaker idea this design briefly held.

> **A culture is not a weighting over the axes. A culture is a set of satisfiers**,
> which is to say what this people actually does about each need.

A weighting is a number. A satisfier is a practice, and a practice can be taught,
argued over, imposed and abandoned. Everything cultural in this model is richer for
the change.

### They are simultaneous, not hierarchical

Unlike Maslow, no need is foundational and none is a luxury. **Subsistence is not
built first because it is not first.**

### They are incommensurable

Sen's capability approach holds that no permanent priority or relative weight can
be assigned between plural human achievements, and he won a Nobel arguing it
against utility maximisation.

⚠ **Incommensurability is a commitment, not an inconvenience.** The moment nine
needs are collapsed into one number so that something has a quantity to maximise,
this model has adopted the position Sen spent a career refuting and has destroyed
the thing that makes cultures different from each other.

**There is no scalar happiness in this model and there will not be one.** That is
charter rule 9, and it is also most of the ethical boundary: because the axes are
never summed, no number exists that says one island is happier than another.

## The rule that keeps nine axes cheap

> **No need may have code of its own.** (charter rule 8)

Nine needs are expensive only if each gets bespoke machinery. They are nearly free
if **a person's needs are a vector, a satisfier is an effect profile over that
vector, and a culture is a set of prescribed satisfiers**. Adding a tenth need is
then data, not a subsystem.

The moment subsistence gets a special case that affection does not have, Maslow's
hierarchy has been smuggled back in through the implementation, and Max-Neef's
whole point is lost to a convenience.

## A satisfier can lie, and that is measurable

Max-Neef classifies satisfiers as **synergic** (meet one need and others with it),
**singular**, **inhibiting** (over-meet one and impair others), **pseudo-satisfiers**
(give a false sense of satisfaction), and **violators** (claim to meet a need while
making it harder to meet, including others).

**This is the mechanism by which a culture can be measurably bad for the persons
carrying it, without anybody declaring from outside which cultures are bad.**

A doctrine prescribing a satisfier that claims to serve identity while destroying
affection is a violator, and violator is a *measurement*: it compares what the
satisfier promises against what the need vector actually does. **Cultural pathology
becomes an observation rather than a judgement**, which is the only form in which
this project can honestly study it.

## Self-determination theory: considered, not adopted as the axes

SDT's three needs, **autonomy, competence and relatedness**, map remarkably onto
machinery this design already requires:

| SDT need | what it already is here |
|---|---|
| competence | does your model predict what actually happens |
| autonomy | are you acting on what you hold yourself, or complying with doctrine |
| relatedness | do those around you believe what you believe |

Not one needs a new substrate, and they are in real tension: relatedness pushes you
to conform, autonomy pushes you to act on your own model, and competence
adjudicates by being the only one the world answers.

**It was not adopted as the axes because it decomposes how a pursuit relates to the
self, rather than what is pursued**, and the second is what a culture prescribes.
It remains the strongest candidate for describing the *quality* of a satisfier once
the axes exist, and its internalisation continuum transfers directly to the
doctrine-to-model pipeline in
[DESIGN_BELIEF_HAS_TWO_LEVELS.md](DESIGN_BELIEF_HAS_TWO_LEVELS.md).

⚠ **Its universality is contested and that is worth knowing before leaning on it.**
The standing critique is that autonomy encodes Western individualism; the standing
defence is that autonomy means acting from your own values, which can include
honouring an obligation. The evidence broadly supports all three needs across
cultures, with competence measurably mattering more in the West. **Choosing any set
of axes is a substantive claim rather than a neutral starting point**, and that is
true of Max-Neef's nine as much as of SDT's three.
